<h2 id="seek-friction">Seek friction</h2>
The internet is dying. Some say it's already dead.

it's just If you loved Web 3.0 (and why wouldn't you: [Web3 is Going Just Great](https://www.web3isgoinggreat.com/)), check it out motherfungibles: Web 4.0 is very fucking nigh. What is Web 4.0 you ask?

Cool.

<x-bluesky-post url="https://bsky.app/profile/geofflemon.bsky.social/post/3mgcotw25os2n" />

Look: I love computer. I need computer. But I crave better relationship with computer? My approach for building a better relationship with computer is seeking not less but more friction (non-sexual).

AI "eliminates friction" and "increases output" and "optimizes throughput" and all that and ok cool sure: We will all have to learn use AI to learn to do my puts better.

But just as computer is tool, AI is tool. And the tools we use determine what kind of tool we become. I want to become a useful tool, wait, shit.

They can try to kill the internet all they want: I still want to connect people, and a mailing list is still the most reliable, ownable way to do that.

I spent a decade-plus lurking on social media, and never wanted to share anything. Now that I am publishing on my own site, I'm not worried about speaking up in the town hall where everyone else is. I just explore the stuff I like to explore, and put it in one place where I can share it with all the people who might connect with it.

Making content for your own website—far from algorithms, with no intent to garner vanity likes with little-to-no genuine/meaningful connection—shifts the _why_ and _how_ for everything I share.

If we're going to revive/rebuild an internet built by/for humans, I think we all need to build our own websites. Please do it. Please share your shit. I will visit your website and I will go "Oh wow that's cool that's what they're up to! Great" and each time I visit I'll poke around and see what else you're doing and go "Oh neat, that too! Wow what a cool, talented friend I have."

God help me: I'm excited to post.

<h2 id="pragmatic-ai">How AI is changing writing and creative work (Podcast)</h2>

It's hard to separate the "AI as gajillion-dollar market darling" conversation from the "AI as internet-ruining slop tsunami" conversation from the "AI as career-swallowing black hole" conversation from the "LLMs as neat and useful tools" conversation.

Earlier this month, I joined [Matt Stauffer](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mattstauffer/), CEO of [Tighten](https://www.linkedin.com/company/tightenco/), on his new podcast "Pragmatic AI" to talk about each of these, but mostly to explore how AI impacts writing and creative work.

<x-youtube-video id="DmtsGXuT7lg" title="How AI is changing writing and creative work" />

We covered a lot: how it's right there in the name that Large Language Models were always going to upend the careers of writers and programmers first; how the uniqueness of your creative process determines the uniqueness of your creative output; why expertise, taste, and curation matter more than ever in the next era of the internet; and why we need more media decentralization with fewer Big Tech chokepoints and gatekeepers.

But mostly we talked about AI as a tool, how tools shape us, and the hopes and fears I have that follow from that.

I'm a professionally curious person. I'm also a sicko: I _like_ the grunt work it takes to understand something and compellingly communicate it. Google Search is functionally useless (a generational own goal), and although I still search daily for ten blue links on UDM14, LLMs are the tools I reach for now when I need to learn faster, follow my curiosity further, and understand more than I need in order to chisel away at the ideas.

I would never let an LLM write sentences for me because writing is the residue of my thought process. Plus, I like making sentences. I prefer the way they taste when I make them.

And that's the Jordan Keller promise—our recipe hasn't changed in 39 years:

    1. Start from your unique domain of expertise, curiosity, tastes, and interests
    2. Notice what you notice
    3. Ask/answer interesting questions
